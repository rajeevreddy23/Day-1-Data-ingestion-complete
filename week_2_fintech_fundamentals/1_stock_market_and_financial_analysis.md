## Part 1: Stock Market Fundamentals Executive Summary

### 1. What is a Stock Market?
A stock market is a centralized marketplace where buyers and sellers trade ownership shares of publicly listed companies. It enables businesses to raise capital for expansion and provides investors with liquidity, wealth creation opportunities, and fractional ownership in enterprise growth.

### 2. Primary Exchanges in India: NSE & BSE
* **National Stock Exchange (NSE):** Founded in 1992, it is India’s largest electronic stock exchange by trading volume, known for its cutting-edge automated screen-based trading and the world’s highest equity derivatives volume.
* **Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE):** Established in 1875 at Dalal Street, Mumbai, BSE is Asia’s oldest stock exchange, hosting over 5,000+ listed companies.

### 3. Benchmark Indices: Nifty 50 & BSE Sensex
* **NIFTY 50:** Managed by NSE Indices, it comprises 50 of the largest and most liquid Indian bluechip stocks across 13 sectors, weighted using free-float market capitalization.
* **BSE SENSEX:** A market-cap weighted index of 30 financially sound and actively traded companies listed on the BSE.

### 4. IPO & SME IPO
* **Initial Public Offering (IPO):** The first sale of stock issued by a company to the public to raise fresh equity capital or allow early promoters/VCs an offer-for-sale (OFS).
* **SME IPO:** Tailored listing platform (NSE Emerge / BSE SME) for Small and Medium Enterprises with relaxed listing criteria (minimum post-issue capital between Rs 1 Cr and Rs 25 Cr) to facilitate growth funding.

### 5. Market Capitalization Segments (SEBI Classification)
\text{Market Capitalization} = \text{Total Outstanding Shares} \times \text{Current Market Price (CMP)}
* **Large Cap:** Top 100 listed companies by market cap (> Rs 20,000 Crore). Characterized by stability, institutional ownership, and resilient balance sheets.
* **Mid Cap:** Ranked 101st to 250th (Rs 5,000 to Rs 20,000 Crore). Higher growth potential accompanied by moderate volatility.
* **Small Cap:** Ranked 251st and below (< Rs 5,000 Crore). High growth runway with heightened liquidity and drawdown risk.

### 6. Key Stock Valuation & Market Metrics
* **Earnings Per Share (EPS):** $\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Profit after Tax} - \text{Preferred Dividends}}{\text{Weighted Average Number of Shares}}$
* **Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E):** Measures how much investors are willing to pay per rupee of earnings: $\text{P/E} = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{EPS}}$
* **Price-to-Book Ratio (P/B):** Compares market value to book/net asset value: $\text{P/B} = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Book Value per Share}}$
* **Dividend & Dividend Yield:** Cash distribution of corporate profits. Yield $= \frac{\text{Annual Dividend per Share}}{\text{Stock Price}} \times 100\%$.
* **Bonus Issue vs. Stock Split:**
  * *Bonus Issue:* Existing shareholders receive additional shares free of cost funded from company reserves (e.g., 1:1 bonus). Face value remains unchanged.
  * *Stock Split:* The nominal face value of existing shares is subdivided (e.g., FV Rs 10 split to FV Rs 2), increasing liquidity without altering reserves.
* **Trading Volume:** The aggregate number of shares transacted over a given trading session, reflecting institutional interest and liquidity.

## Part 2: Financial Statements & Ratio Analysis

### The Three Financial Statements:
1. **Balance Sheet (Statement of Financial Position):** Snapshot of what a company owns (Assets) and owes (Liabilities) at a specific date: $\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \text{Shareholders\' Equity}$.
2. **Profit & Loss Statement (Income Statement):** Measures financial performance over a reporting period: $\text{Revenue} - \text{Operating Expenses} = \text{EBITDA} \rightarrow \text{EBIT} \rightarrow \text{EBT} \rightarrow \text{PAT (Net Income)}$.
3. **Cash Flow Statement:** Reconciles accounting net profit with actual liquidity movement across **Operating Activities (CFO)**, **Investing Activities (CFI)**, and **Financing Activities (CFF)**.
